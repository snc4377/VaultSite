Many large organizations acquire new companies over time and integrate them. One way to do this to facilitate usage is to establish a trust relationship with the new domain. This way, migrating all established objects can be avoided, which greatly speeds up integration. This trust can also introduce weaknesses into the client's environment if not handled carefully. A subdomain with an exploitable flaw or vulnerability can provide us with a fast path to the target domain. Companies may also establish trusts with other companies (such as a Managed Service Provider or MSP), a customer, or other business units within the same company (like a division of the company in another geographic region). Let's explore domain trust relationships further and how we can abuse built-in functionality during our assessments.

---

## Overview of Domain Trust Relationships

A [trust relationship (trust)](https://social.technet.microsoft.com/wiki/contents/articles/50969.active-directory-forest-trust-attention-points.aspx) is used to establish authentication between forests (forest-forest) or between domains (domain-domain or intra-domain), allowing users to access resources (or perform administrative tasks) in another domain outside their primary domain where their account resides. A trust creates a link between the authentication systems of two domains and can enable one-way or two-way communication. An organization can create several types of trusts:

- `Parent-child`: Two or more domains within the same forest. The child domain has a transitive two-way trust relationship with the parent domain, meaning users in the child domain `corp.inlanefreight.local` could authenticate in the parent domain `inlanefreight.local`, and vice versa.
- `Cross-link`: A trust between child domains to speed up authentication.
- `External`: A non-transitive trust between two separate domains in different forests that are not already joined by a forest trust. This type of trust uses [SID filtering (SID filtering)](https://www.serverbrain.org/active-directory-2008/sid-history-and-sid-filtering.html) or filters authentication requests (by SID) that do not originate from the trusted domain.
- `Tree root`: A transitive two-way trust between a forest root domain and a new tree root domain. These are created by design when setting up a new tree root domain within a forest.
- `Forest`: A transitive trust between two forest root domains.
- [ESAE](https://docs.microsoft.com/en-us/security/compass/esae-retirement): A bastion forest used to manage Active Directory.

When establishing a trust, certain elements may be modified based on the business case.

Trusts can be transitive or non-transitive.

- A `transitive` trust means the trust extends to objects trusted by the child domain. For example, say we have three domains. In a transitive relationship, if `Domain A` has a trust with `Domain B`, and `Domain B` has a `transitive` trust with `Domain C`, then `Domain A` will automatically trust `Domain C`.
- In a `non-transitive trust`, only the child domain itself is trusted.

#### Trust Comparison Table

|Transitive|Non-Transitive|
|---|---|
|Shared, one-to-many|Direct trust|
|Trust is shared with anyone in the forest|Does not extend to child domains at the next level|
|Forest, tree root, parent-child, and cross-link trusts are transitive|Typical for external or custom trust configurations|

An easy comparison is package delivery to your home. For a `transitive` trust, you've extended permission to anyone in your household (forest) to accept a package on your behalf. For a `non-transitive` trust, you've given strict orders with the package that only the delivery service and you can handle the package, and only you can sign for it.

Trusts can be configured in two directions: one-way or two-way.

- `One-way trust`: Users in a `trusted` domain can access resources in a trusting domain, but not vice versa.
- `Two-way trust`: Users in both trusting domains can access each other's resources. For example, in a two-way trust between `INLANEFREIGHT.LOCAL` and `FREIGHTLOGISTICS.LOCAL`, users of `INLANEFREIGHT.LOCAL` could access resources in `FREIGHTLOGISTICS.LOCAL`, and vice versa.

Domain trust relationships are often misconfigured and can provide us with unintended critical attack paths. Additionally, trusts established for ease of use may not be later reviewed for potential security implications if security is not considered before establishing the trust relationship. A Merger and Acquisition (M&A) between two companies may result in two-way trusts with acquired companies, which can unknowingly introduce risks into the acquiring company's environment if the acquired company's security posture is unknown and untested. If someone wanted to attack your organization, they might also look at the other company you acquired as a potentially softer target to attack, allowing them to enter your organization indirectly. It's not uncommon to perform an attack like Kerberoasting against a domain outside the primary domain and obtain a user with administrative access within the primary domain. I've performed many penetration tests where this was the case: I couldn't find a foothold in the primary domain, but I could find a flaw in a trusted domain that, in turn, gave me a foothold or even full admin rights in the primary domain. This type of "indirect" attack could be prevented if security is considered paramount before establishing any domain trust. As we examine trust relationships, keep these ideas in mind for reporting. We'll often find that the larger organization is unaware a trust relationship exists with one or more domains.

## Enumerating Trust Relationships

We can use the [Get-ADTrust](https://docs.microsoft.com/en-us/powershell/module/activedirectory/get-adtrust?view=windowsserver2022-ps) cmdlet to enumerate domain trust relationships. This is especially useful if we're limited to using built-in tools only.

The output above shows that our current domain `INLANEFREIGHT.LOCAL` has two domain trusts. The first is with `LOGISTICS.INLANEFREIGHT.LOCAL`, and the `IntraForest` property indicates that this is a child domain, and we are currently positioned in the forest root domain. The second trust is with the domain `FREIGHTLOGISTICS.LOCAL`, and the `ForestTransitive` property is set to `True`, meaning this is a forest trust or an external trust. We can see that both trusts are configured to be bidirectional, which means users can authenticate back and forth across both trusts. It is important to take note of this during an assessment. If we cannot authenticate through a trust, we will not be able to perform any enumeration or attacks through it.
